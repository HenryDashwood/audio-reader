Hearful — Privacy Policy

Last updated 19 August 2026

Hearful is a podcast and article player you control by speaking. It is built for people who cannot easily use a screen, which means the microphone is not an optional extra here — it is the main way the app is used. This policy explains what that involves.

The short version

  • We keep your email address, the shows you follow, and how far through each episode you are.
  • Only after you allow AI data sharing, what you say is turned into text and sent to OpenRouter and an AI model provider so the app can work out which episode you meant. The text of your request is sent, never a recording.
  • That request is sent along with a list of recent episodes from the shows you follow, so the AI has something to choose between.
  • A record of each request, including the words you said, is kept for 30 days so that mistakes can be found and put right.
  • The app also reports how well its own microphone and speech recognition worked, so that times it could not hear you can be found and fixed. That report contains timings only — never your words, and never any audio.
  • Diagnostic records use a random code instead of your account identifier. Deleting your account destroys the only mapping to that code.
  • There is no advertising, no cross-app tracking, and nothing is sold or shared with data brokers.
  • You can delete your account, and everything in it, from inside the app.

Who is responsible for your data

The data controller is Henry Dashwood. You can reach us at hcndashwood@gmail.com about anything on this page.

What we collect, and why

  • Your email address and an Apple account identifier. These come from Sign in with Apple when you create an account. If you chose to hide your email, we only ever see Apple's private relay address. We use these to know which account is yours and nothing else.
  • The shows you subscribe to. So your library is there when you open the app.
  • Your place in each episode. So playback resumes where you stopped, including on another device.
  • A sign-in token. Stored only as a one-way hash, so that even we cannot read it back. It expires after 180 days without use.
  • An Apple refresh token. Encrypted, and kept for one purpose only: so that deleting your account can also tell Apple to revoke the Sign in with Apple connection.
  • Your AI data-sharing choice. We keep the version of the explanation you accepted and when you accepted it. This proves which choice you made and lets us ask again if what is shared changes materially.
  • A random diagnostic identifier. This is generated for your account and is different from your account identifier. The mapping exists only in your account row and is deleted with the account.

When you speak to the app

Before Hearful sends anything to an AI provider, the app explains what will be shared and asks you to choose Allow AI Data Sharing. If you choose Not Now, nothing is sent to an AI provider. You can allow it later, or turn it off again, under Settings → Privacy & Support. Turning it off stops future AI requests immediately; the rest of the on-screen app remains available.

Speech is turned into text on your device, using Apple's speech recognition. If your device does not have the on-device speech models available, Apple's system falls back to processing the audio on Apple's servers — that part is handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy, not by us.

The resulting text is sent to our server. We do not receive, store or transmit any audio recording of your voice at any point.

To work out which episode you meant, our server sends that text to an AI language-model provider — OpenRouter, which routes the request to a model provider on our behalf. Alongside your words, we send a list of recent episodes from the shows you follow: their titles, the show names, publication dates, and a short extract of each description. The AI needs that list in order to have something to choose between.

In plain terms: the AI provider sees what you asked for, and part of your listening library. It does not see your name, your email address, or your account identifier. If you ask to follow a publication we do not recognise, the name you said is also used to search the web for its feed.

Every request tells OpenRouter to use only a model endpoint that declares zero data retention and does not collect the request for training or other non-transient use. If no such endpoint is available, the request fails rather than being sent under a weaker data policy.

Keeping the app working

An app you drive by speaking is only as good as its understanding of you, and a request that plays the wrong thing looks, from the outside, exactly like one that worked. So that mistakes can be found again and put right, our server keeps a record of each request with Pydantic Logfire, a diagnostics service whose servers are in the European Union.

That record holds the words you said, which episode the app decided on, how long it took, and anything that went wrong along the way. It is labelled with the random diagnostic identifier described above — never your account identifier, name or email address, and never your device's IP address, which is removed before the record leaves our server. It is deleted after 30 days.

The app on your phone sends a short record of its own each time you use the microphone — including the times it went wrong, which our server would otherwise never hear about at all. If the app cannot hear you, no voice command or transcript reaches our server; only this separate, word-free diagnostic record reports the failure.

That record says how long the app listened, whether the microphone started working, which speech recogniser it used, whether VoiceOver was running, and what kind of thing you asked for — playing something, pausing, setting a sleep timer. It never contains your words and never any audio. If your phone is offline it is kept on the device and sent later. That queue is kept separately for each signed-in account and is deleted when you sign out or delete the account. It is held alongside the records above, for the same 30 days, and used the same way.

If the app crashes or freezes, your phone's own diagnostics — Apple's MetricKit — hand us a report of where in the app it happened, which we forward to the same service. These contain no personal information: they describe the app's own code, not you or what you were listening to. Your phone only provides them if you have left iOS's Share With App Developers analytics setting on, and you can turn it off at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.

It is used to fix the app and for nothing else. It does not build a profile of you, it is not shared onward, and no advertising or cross-app tracking is involved in it.

What other companies can see

  • Apple — sign-in, and speech recognition where it falls back to Apple's servers.
  • OpenRouter and the model provider it routes to — your spoken requests as text, and the episode list described above.
  • Railway — our hosting provider, which runs the servers and database where your account is stored. Like any hosting network, it also processes connection information such as your device's IP address while it delivers a request. Hearful does not put that address into its own diagnostic records.
  • Pydantic Logfire — our diagnostics service, which holds the record of each request described above for 30 days.
  • Podcast publishers and their hosts — episode audio, artwork and pictures inside articles are fetched by the app directly from wherever the publisher hosts them, so those servers see your device's IP address, exactly as they would in a browser or podcast app. Article text and podcast search are fetched by our server instead. Article pictures use a temporary, non-persistent browser data store in Hearful.

What we do not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No advertising analytics or tracking across other apps or websites.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data with data brokers.
  • No storing of voice recordings.

How long we keep things

Your account, subscriptions and playback positions are kept until you delete your account. Sign-in tokens expire after 180 days without use. Shows that nobody subscribes to and nobody has listened to are removed from our catalogue after 30 days. The diagnostic records described above are deleted after 30 days.

Turning off AI data sharing prevents new AI requests. It does not rewrite diagnostic records already created while the feature was allowed; those continue to expire under the same 30-day limit.

We also keep backups of the database, so that the service can be recovered if something goes wrong with it. This means a backup taken before you deleted your account may still hold a copy of your data for a short time afterwards. Backups are never kept longer than 30 days, and are not used for any purpose other than restoring the service.

Deleting your account

Open Settings in the app and choose Delete Account. This permanently removes your account, your subscriptions and your place in every episode, and asks Apple to revoke the Sign in with Apple connection. It also deletes the only mapping between your account and its random diagnostic identifier. Existing diagnostic records then remain only under that random code until their 30-day expiry and are no longer connected through Hearful's live account database. A retained disaster-recovery backup may still contain the old mapping until that backup's 30-day expiry, but backups are used only to restore the service. Because a spoken transcript is free text, its own words could still identify you if you chose to say identifying information. It cannot be undone. You do not need to contact us or wait for us to act.

Your rights

If you are in the UK or the EU, you have the right to access your data, to correct it, to have it deleted, to object to how it is used, and to receive a copy of it. Deletion is available immediately in the app; for anything else, write to us at the address above and we will respond within one month. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

We use different lawful bases for different purposes. Account, library, playback and the processing needed to answer a voice request are necessary to provide the service you ask for. We separately require the explicit in-app permission described above before sharing a request with third-party AI, and you can withdraw that permission at any time. Limited security, reliability and accessibility diagnostics are used for our legitimate interest in keeping Hearful safe and usable: they are pseudonymised, kept for 30 days, never used for advertising or profiling, and you may object by contacting us. Crash diagnostics are also controlled by iOS's Share With App Developers setting.

Children

Hearful is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

Changes to this policy

If we change how any of this works, we will update this page and change the date at the top. Material changes will be announced in the app.
